Cybernetics, Gubernatorial, and Kubernetes

I recently stumbled across an interesting etymology. Cybernetics, Gubernatorial, and Kubernetes all come from the same root.

Kubernetes itself is actually closest to the original Greek word κυβερνήτης (kybernḗtēs) meaning "helmsman", or someone who steers a ship. The Greeks themselves extended the meaning to the metaphor of a leader guiding a state.

It then went to Latin, as the word "gubernator" which still means helmsman, and also, somewhat predictably "governor".

Then it split, going through French as "governer", before coming to English as "governor".

However US English in the early 1700s, grabbed it straight from the Latin as "gubernatorial".

This is fascinating, but what about cybernetics?

In 1948, mathematician Norbert Wiener (ha, ha) published Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine.

He created the name "cybernetics" from the Greek kybernḗtēs, and used it to mean a feedback control mechanism. And it makes sense.

A helmsman:

  • Has a destination
  • Observes the current direction
  • Adjusts the steering
  • Waits the correct length of time
  • Observes the new direction
  • Rinse and repeat

There's an important meta-feedback step there, that you have to learn the correct amount of time to wait for feedback, which you do by observing how quickly feedback occurred before. It is one of the key things you see in eg learner drivers, and why driving slowly can be more difficult than driving quickly.

Apparently Wiener (ha ha), wasn't the first to coin cybernetics. The French had a term cybernétique in the 1800s to describe the science of government, but the French don't count.

Yeah, yeah, but what about cybersex?

Well, like with most sexual innovation, it came about because of Doctor Who. In the 1960s Doctor Who invented "cybermen". The term "cyborg" had already been used, but not by anyone interesting. Doctor Who popularised the term "cybermen", followed by William Gibson coining the term "cyberspace", and after that everything was cyber. (🤔 Adam Buxton should do a podcast called "Cyber with Rosie").

Oh and then Kubernetes came along and recaptured the original Greek meaning, and has gone hard using terms related to on the original meaning: helm, harbour, fleet, etc.

And apparently the correct Greek pronunciation would be more like "kee-ver-NEE-tees"
